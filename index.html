I am a PhD candidate in economics at the Paris School of Economics and EHESS, supervised by Ekaterina Zhuravskaya. In 2024–25 I was a visiting PhD student in the Department of Politics at Princeton University, hosted by Jacob N. Shapiro.

My main research interests are Political Economics and Media Economics. In particular, my research investigates how new information technologies shape news demand, political beliefs and behaviour, and mental health. I use quasi-experimental methods on large-scale administrative and behavioural data.

I am on the economics job market in 2026–27.

Full abstract

How does TikTok use affect news consumption? Leveraging detailed device-level mobile browsing and app use data and a shock to early adoption of TikTok as a novel, plausibly exogenous source of variation, this paper estimates the causal effect of TikTok use on mobile news consumption in the United States. I find that increased TikTok use substantially reduces news consumption related to major news events such as election weeks, national disasters and major political developments. TikTok use causes decreases along both the extensive and intensive margins. It also causes a decrease in the length of individuals’ news consumption sessions, suggesting that reduced attention spans contribute to the effect. Survey evidence shows that the effect is not limited to mobile devices and has downstream consequences: high-TikTok-use media markets show decreases in traditional news consumption and political knowledge, and increases in partisan polarization in presidential approval, after TikTok’s proliferation.
